\section{Key Finding}

The PIV pipeline is \textbf{not FFT-bound}---it is \textbf{warp-bound}. The predictor-corrector step dominates passes~2--4, consuming \textbf{55--87\%} of per-pass runtime. The FFT cross-correlation (\texttt{bulkxcorr2d}) accounts for only 5--20\% of later passes. The predictor-corrector uses thread-parallel \texttt{cv2.remap} via a class-level \texttt{ThreadPoolExecutor} sized to \texttt{omp\_threads}, with per-image mesh computation fused into each warp task to avoid large intermediate allocations.

\section{Optimisation Impact (25\,MP, 8 pairs, 1 worker)}

Two optimisations were applied to the predictor-corrector: (1)~thread-parallel \texttt{cv2.remap} loops via a class-level \texttt{ThreadPoolExecutor(omp\_threads)} with \texttt{cv2.setNumThreads(1)}, and (2)~fusing the mesh coordinate computation into each warp thread to eliminate ${\sim}$3\,GB of intermediate allocations per pass.

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item The pipeline is \textbf{warp-bound}, not FFT-bound. Predictor-corrector consumes 55--87\% of passes~2--4.
\item Predictor-corrector cost is \textbf{$O(\text{pixels})$}, nearly constant across passes; per-window costs (xcorr, outlier, infill) scale with window count.
\item At 25\,MP with 8 pairs, the per-pair processing rate is \textbf{1.86\,s/pair} across all 4 passes (4 threads).
\item The fused mesh+warp section dominates at 36\% of pass~4 (25\,MP). Further gains would require moving this computation to C/CUDA.
\item Outlier detection becomes significant at fine grids: 18\% of pass~4 at 25\,MP (574$\times$663 = 380k windows).
\end{enumerate}
