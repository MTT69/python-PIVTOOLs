\section{Key Finding}

The pipeline processes 4\,MP images at \textbf{0.31\,s/pair} and 25\,MP images at \textbf{1.76\,s/pair} across all 4 passes with 4 threads. It is \textbf{warp-bound}, not FFT-bound: the predictor-corrector step (\texttt{cv2.remap} image warping) dominates passes~2--4 at \textbf{54--87\%} of per-pass runtime, while the FFT cross-correlation (\texttt{bulkxcorr2d}) accounts for only 12--26\%.

\section{Why It's This Fast: Threading}

Without threading, the same pipeline takes \textbf{0.68\,s/pair} at 4\,MP---a 2.2$\times$ slowdown. A class-level \texttt{ThreadPoolExecutor(4)} parallelises GIL-releasing operations across the image pairs in a batch. \texttt{cv2.setNumThreads(1)} prevents OpenCV internal threading. The pool and OpenMP (used by \texttt{bulkxcorr2d}) never run concurrently---no contention by design.

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item Per-pair throughput: \textbf{0.31\,s} at 4\,MP, \textbf{1.76\,s} at 25\,MP (4 threads, all 4 passes).
\item The pipeline is \textbf{warp-bound}, not FFT-bound. Predictor-corrector (\texttt{cv2.remap}) consumes 54--87\% of passes~2--4. FFT cross-correlation is only 12--26\%.
\item Predictor-corrector cost is $O(\text{pixels})$, nearly constant across passes~2--4. Per-window costs (xcorr, outlier, infill) scale with window count.
\item Threading delivers \textbf{2.2$\times$ speedup} because \texttt{cv2.remap} and \texttt{scipy.ndimage} fully release the GIL. Without it, throughput drops to 0.68\,s/pair at 4\,MP.
\item Use \texttt{local\_median} for mid-pass infilling (${\sim}$8$\times$ faster than biharmonic, threads at 2.1$\times$). Reserve biharmonic for final pass only.
\item The fused mesh+warp dominates at 36--39\% of pass~4. Further gains require moving this to C/CUDA.
\end{enumerate}
